2. Visiting this website

This is a static website: it has no user accounts, no comment section and no forms, and we do not run any analytics or advertising tools.

Hosting. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. When you open a page, your browser necessarily sends technical data to GitHub’s servers: your IP address, the date and time, the page requested, and your browser and operating system (the “user agent”). GitHub uses this data to deliver the website and to keep it secure, for example against attacks, and keeps it in server logs according to its own privacy statement. We do not receive these logs and cannot use them to identify you.

  • Purpose: showing you the website and keeping it secure and available.
  • Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running a secure, working website (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • Recipient: GitHub, Inc., as our hosting provider. On transfers to the USA, see section 9.

Fonts. The fonts on this site (Manrope, Unbounded, JetBrains Mono and Caveat, all under the SIL Open Font License) are stored on our own hosting. Opening a page does not connect you to Google Fonts or any other font service.

Security. The website is only served over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

3. Cookies and similar technologies

This website does not set any cookies and does not use local storage, fingerprinting or any other way of storing or reading information on your device. That is why there is no cookie banner: none is needed.

The only exception is a YouTube video that you choose to play (see section 4). Pressing play is your consent to that one video loading, as required by Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC) and Article 399 of the Polish Electronic Communications Law (Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej).

4. YouTube videos

Some project pages show videos hosted on YouTube. We use a “click to load” solution: until you press play, the page shows only a placeholder and no data is sent to YouTube or Google. When you press play, the video loads from YouTube’s privacy-enhanced domain (youtube-nocookie.com).

From that moment, Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland, receives your IP address and technical information about your device and browser, and may store cookies or similar data on your device to play and measure the video. Google is responsible for that processing under its own privacy policy, and data may be transferred to Google LLC in the USA (see section 9).

  • Legal basis: your consent, given by pressing play (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR and the provisions on terminal equipment in section 3).
  • Withdrawing consent: simply don’t press play, or delete YouTube/Google cookies in your browser settings. Withdrawal does not affect what happened before it.
  • If you are under 16, please ask a parent or guardian before playing videos.

5. Links to other websites and social media

The website contains ordinary links to other services, for example Facebook and Instagram (Meta Platforms Ireland Limited, Merrion Road, Dublin 4, Ireland), Google Drive folders with project materials (Google Ireland Limited), local news websites, and our partners’ websites. These are plain links, not embedded plugins: nothing is sent to those services until you click. Once you are on their website, their own privacy policies apply.

Our Facebook and Instagram pages. When you visit our pages on Facebook or Instagram, Meta processes your data under its own policies. For the page statistics Meta provides to us (“Page Insights”), we and Meta Platforms Ireland Limited are joint controllers under Article 26 GDPR. Meta has taken on primary responsibility for this processing and for handling your rights in the Page Insights Controller Addendum. We only see anonymous, aggregated statistics. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in communicating about our work (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

6. When you contact us

If you write to us by email, by post, or through Facebook or Instagram, we process the data you give us: usually your name, contact details, the content of your message and, on social media, your profile name.

  • Purpose: answering you and handling your request.
  • Legal basis: our legitimate interest in replying to messages (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). If you ask about joining a project, steps taken at your request before an agreement (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • How long: as long as needed to deal with your matter, and then no longer than 12 months after the conversation ends, unless the matter continues (for example, you join a project; see section 8) or we must keep it to establish or defend legal claims.
  • Recipients: our email provider [name of email provider, e.g. Google Workspace or Microsoft 365] acting on our behalf (Article 28 GDPR), and Meta for messages sent on its platforms.
  • Giving us your data is voluntary, but we cannot reply without contact details.

7. Photos and videos of people on this website

To show and report on our projects, this website publishes photos and videos from our events, which may show participants, facilitators and audiences.

  • Purpose: documenting and promoting our projects, including the publicity obligations of projects co-funded by the European Union.
  • Legal basis: the consent of the people shown (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR and Article 81 of the Polish Act on Copyright and Related Rights). For people under 18, consent is given by a parent or legal guardian. Where a person only appears as a detail of a larger whole, such as a crowd at a public event, Polish law allows publication without individual consent; in these cases our legal basis is our legitimate interest in documenting public events (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • How long: until consent is withdrawn or an objection is upheld, or until the material is no longer relevant to our activities.
  • Your choice: if you appear in a photo or video and want it removed or blurred, contact us (section 1) and we will act promptly. Withdrawing consent does not affect publication before the withdrawal.

8. Taking part in our projects

When you apply for or take part in one of our projects, such as an Erasmus+ youth exchange, we receive more information about you, for example your name, date of birth, contact details, nationality, travel details, emergency contact, and, if you share it, information about your health or dietary needs so we can keep you safe. You receive detailed information about this processing when you register. In short:

  • Purposes: organising the project (travel, accommodation, insurance, safety, certificates such as Youthpass) and reporting to the funders.
  • Legal bases: the participation agreement (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR); our legal obligations under the grant agreement and accounting law (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR); and, for health information, your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR).
  • Recipients: for Erasmus+ projects, the Polish National Agency, the Foundation for the Development of the Education System (Fundacja Rozwoju Systemu Edukacji, FRSE), and the European Commission, through the official Erasmus+ IT tools; partner organisations in the project; and service providers such as accommodation, transport and insurance companies, only as far as needed.
  • How long: as long as the grant agreement and the law require. For Erasmus+ projects, this is usually 5 years after the final payment of the grant (3 years for grants up to EUR 60,000), plus any longer period required by Polish accounting and tax law.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • Access your data and get a copy (Article 15)
  • Correct inaccurate or incomplete data (Article 16)
  • Have your data deleted (“right to be forgotten”, Article 17)
  • Restrict processing (Article 18)
  • Data portability: receive your data in a machine-readable format (Article 20)
  • Object to processing based on our legitimate interests (Article 21)
  • Withdraw consent at any time, without affecting processing before the withdrawal (Article 7(3))

To use your rights, contact us using the details in section 1. It is free. We will reply within one month, which can be extended by two further months for complex requests; we will tell you if that happens (Article 12(3) GDPR). We may ask you to confirm your identity first.

We do not use automated decision-making or profiling that has legal or similarly significant effects on you (Article 22 GDPR).

11. Right to complain

If you think we process your data unlawfully, you can complain to a supervisory authority (Article 77 GDPR). In Poland, this is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, UODO), ul. Stawki 2, 00-193 Warszawa, uodo.gov.pl. You can also complain in the EU country where you live or work. We would appreciate the chance to address your concerns first, so please feel free to contact us.

12. Other information

Is providing data required? No. You can browse the website without giving us any personal data. Contacting us and taking part in projects is voluntary, though we need certain information to reply to you or to organise a project.

Children. This website is not directed at children under 16, and we do not knowingly collect their data through it. For minors taking part in our projects, a parent or legal guardian is involved as described in section 8.

Changes to this notice. We may update this notice when our website or activities change. The date at the top shows the latest version.
